**You can build and sell commercial products using Lloyal.**

These examples explain the [Developer Grant](https://github.com/lloyal-ai/hdk/blob/8678ebcd02c425b9e569d97e58063e2af143ffb7/GRANT.md); they do not replace
its governing terms or limit permission to the use cases listed here. The
[Licensing FAQ](https://docs.lloyal.ai/licensing/faq) summarizes the boundaries.

## Permitted

### Commercial applications

- A paid intelligent inbox app sold on the Mac App Store.
- A spreadsheet with AI enrichment, sold to enterprises with per-seat pricing.
- An on-device assistant embedded in a medical device or car infotainment system.
- A deep-research product that competes directly with Fieldnote.
- A workflow product whose users compose agents inside that application.
- A hosted product whose backend uses Lloyal to serve its own end users.

The grant permits applications in categories Lloyal occupies now or enters in
the future. It covers desktop, mobile, CLI, server and embedded applications.

### Abilities and application extensions

- Building, selling and publishing an ability through the canonical channel.
- Distributing abilities privately within your organization.
- Distributing abilities to your own customers as part of your application.
- Running a private registry or mirror for that distribution.
- Offering your application's own plugin or extension mechanism in its own format.

An application's own plugin system is expressly outside the general ability
marketplace boundary in Section 4(c).

### Services, internal use and research

- Building a custom application for a client and deploying it on the client's
  infrastructure.
- Hosting that application to serve its own users.
- Using the runtime internally for search, document review or other business tasks.
- Studying, modifying or contributing to the runtime.
- Using it for non-commercial education or research.

Required license and copyright notices still apply when redistributing the
covered software.

## Competing developer-platform uses

During a version's FSL period, Section 4 limits the competing-use boundary to
commercially making the covered software available as:

- A substitute framework, runtime, SDK or library whose primary purpose is
  enabling third-party developers to build and ship their own agentic AI apps.
- A managed or hosted runtime service for third-party developers.
- A general alternative registry or marketplace for HDK abilities offered to
  third-party harness developers.

An application whose users compose agents within it remains an application.
The framework boundary concerns exposing the Software's developer APIs as the
primary purpose so third parties can build and ship their own separate apps.

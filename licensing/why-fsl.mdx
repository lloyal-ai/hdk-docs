---
title: "Why FSL"
description: "How FSL and the Developer Grant distinguish applications built with Lloyal from competing developer platforms."
---

New versions of Lloyal's runtime use **FSL-1.1-MIT** with the
[Developer Grant](https://github.com/lloyal-ai/hdk/blob/d9112692d592f73e3922d096a4cdf2edcc889089/GRANT.md). The license provides access to the source and
an irrevocable MIT license for each version on the second anniversary
of the date that version is made available. The grant adds explicit permissions
for application and ability developers during the FSL period.

## Applications and abilities remain permitted

The grant permits building, distributing, selling, licensing and hosting
applications and abilities, including products that compete with Lloyal's own
applications. This protection is not limited to particular industries or
business models. Hosting your own application and operating its own plugin
system are permitted.

Internal ability distribution and distribution to your own customers as part of
your application are also permitted. An app that lets its users compose agents
within it remains an application under the grant.

## The boundary is the developer platform

The grant limits competing-use restrictions to substitute developer frameworks
or runtimes, runtime-as-a-service offered to third-party developers, and general
alternative ability registries offered to third-party harness developers.

This boundary protects Lloyal's developer-platform business while preserving
the right to build and commercialize applications on it.

## Each new version becomes MIT after two years

The two-year transition is in the license. It applies separately to each
released version; a new release does not reset the clock on an earlier one.
The Developer Grant is also irrevocable for versions published while it is in
effect. Future grant revisions do not remove their existing permissions.

Versions previously published under **FSL-1.1-Apache-2.0** keep their existing
terms, future Apache 2.0 rights and conversion dates. The migration does not
replace their existing grant permissions. Each version's bundled `LICENSE`
identifies the future license that applies to it.

## The grant supplements the standard license

Lloyal keeps the FSL text unmodified and carries the additional permissions in
`GRANT.md`. The grant only adds permissions. If the grant and license can be
read differently, the reading more permissive to the developer controls.

The CLI and Fieldnote application source use MIT, while
`@lloyal-labs/channel-verify` uses Apache 2.0. They do not need the Developer
Grant for their own code. Third-party dependencies and model weights retain
their own licenses.
